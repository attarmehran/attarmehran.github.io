01Secure control2026

Can a system stay safe—and keep tracking—during an attack?

Use a graded response: preserve tracking when it is safe, and hand control to a local fallback when it is not.

The problem

Corrupted measurements or commands can push a constrained physical system outside its safe operating region. Switching immediately to an emergency controller can also sacrifice useful tracking performance.

The main idea

Learn worst-case reachable sets from data. Combine an anomaly detector and a controller-side tracking supervisor with plant-side command verification and emergency control. When measurements become unreliable, supervised open-loop tracking can continue while its safety conditions hold.

What the paper shows

The author manuscript presents safety arguments and a simulation example showing how the architecture protects constraints while retaining tracking where feasible.

Scope & limitations

The analysis concerns constrained linear systems, bounded disturbances, sufficiently informative data, a suitable tracking controller, and trusted local safety components. It does not guarantee uninterrupted tracking under every attack.

02Secure control2024 online · 2025 issue

What should happen when a network command becomes unsafe?

Give the plant a local safety check and an emergency controller that can act independently of the network.

The problem

A remote controller may receive manipulated measurements or send a command that is changed in transit. Detecting an anomaly alone does not keep the physical process within its constraints.

The main idea

A data-driven detector checks measurement consistency. At the plant, a safety module tests commands using worst-case reachable sets. Unsafe commands are replaced by a local set-theoretic MPC action that moves the state toward a robust invariant region.

What the paper shows

Two-tank simulations illustrate constraint preservation under attacks on measurements and actuation, including a case where the detector’s alarm is compromised.

Scope & limitations

The guarantee depends on linear dynamics, bounded uncertainty, informative data, a certified feasible region, and trusted plant-side measurements and control. Emergency operation prioritizes safety; it can interrupt reference tracking.

03Secure control2023

Which states can safely reach the target without a known model?

Work backward from a safe target to learn where a controller can act with guarantees.

The problem

Robust MPC needs to know which states can reach a target despite disturbances. Standard set computations usually require a known dynamical model.

The main idea

Construct conservative zonotopic inner approximations from noisy trajectories in an extended state–input space. Compute a family of regions offline; online, select admissible inputs using those regions without revisiting the raw data or an explicit system model.

What the paper shows

The paper establishes recursive admissibility and compares the controller with a model-based counterpart in simulation. The illustrated data-driven controller reaches the terminal region, with additional steps due to conservative sets.

Scope & limitations

The method assumes linear dynamics, known disturbance bounds, sufficiently informative data, and an appropriate terminal region. Inner approximations can exclude feasible states and reduce performance.

04Secure control2023

Can changing the transmitted data expose a stealthy attack?

Change the measurement representation so an attacker cannot simply reuse a complete, unchanged view of the plant.

The problem

Replay and covert attacks can be constructed to evade conventional detectors by making manipulated signals look consistent with normal operation.

The main idea

Encode sensor measurements using a randomly changing lower-dimensional PCA representation. Reconstruct measurements at the receiver, estimate states and unknown inputs with an observer, and use the estimated unknown inputs in a Gaussian anomaly detector.

What the paper shows

The analysis establishes detection properties for the considered replay and covert attacks. Quadruple-tank simulations demonstrate the architecture and compare attack responses.

Scope & limitations

The results depend on the specified system, observer, encoding, uncertainty, and attacker assumptions. Reconstruction introduces an error that must be accounted for. The paper studies detection; automatic attack mitigation is a separate problem.

05Robotics & estimation2018 online · 2019 issue

How can an observer estimate dynamics that are not fully known?

Estimate the system state and a lumped disturbance together, using a principled time-varying design.

The problem

Unknown nonlinear dynamics and disturbances make state estimation difficult. Observer tuning must also control the size of transient and persistent errors.

The main idea

Combine differential algebraic spectral theory with a spectral Lyapunov function to design a time-varying extended state observer. Time-varying parallel differential eigenvalues guide the observer’s bandwidth and parameters.

What the paper shows

The publisher abstract reports estimation-error analysis, comparisons on the TMUBOT quadruped model, and a practical implementation on the robot.

Scope & limitations

This overview is based on the publisher abstract. It does not reproduce the theorem assumptions, tuning procedure, or quantitative results; consult the article for those details.

06Learning2019

How can an agent improve its decisions through experience?

Connect the action–reward loop to Q-learning, then move from a table of values to feature-based approximation.

The problem

An agent acting in an uncertain environment needs to learn which actions produce useful long-term outcomes.

The main idea

The tutorial introduces Markov decision processes, temporal-difference learning, Q-learning, and approximate Q-learning. Worked gridworld and Pacman examples connect the definitions to updates of action values.

What the paper shows

Its contribution is educational: an accessible path from basic concepts to example learning procedures. It is a tutorial preprint, rather than a new algorithm with a performance benchmark.

Scope & limitations

Tabular methods grow with the state–action space; function approximation introduces dependence on the chosen features. The examples do not establish general safety or convergence guarantees for arbitrary dynamical systems.

07Robotics & estimation2016

Can joint-level observers handle an uncertain, coupled robot?

Treat coupling and unknown dynamics as quantities to estimate, while varying observer gains to reduce the initial error peak.

The problem

A two-joint manipulator has interacting dynamics and uncertainty. Decentralized design is simpler, but the effects of coupling still need to be handled.

The main idea

Use a time-varying extended state observer to estimate the states and combined unknown dynamics for each subsystem, including disturbances associated with joint coupling.

What the paper shows

The author-uploaded abstract reports simulation comparisons in which time-varying gains reduce the peaking of estimation error relative to the compared observers.

Scope & limitations

The accessible summary supports a simulation-based result for the studied two-degree-of-freedom manipulator. It does not establish experimental validation or universal superiority across robot designs.

08Robotics & estimation2016

How can a quadruped correct its posture when the ground tilts?

Fuse complementary sensor signals, then use the estimated orientation to adjust the supporting legs.

The problem

Gyroscope bias and body acceleration complicate attitude estimation. An inaccurate estimate can undermine the robot’s posture correction.

The main idea

Fuse gyroscope, accelerometer, and magnetometer data with a switching extended Kalman filter. Change the measurement model between accelerated and non-accelerated motion, estimate orientation and gyro bias, and compensate body deviation through the support legs.

What the paper shows

The paper reports a simulation comparison with a nonlinear observer and body-attitude correction on a platform subject to ramp disturbances.

Scope & limitations

The reported task concerns attitude estimation and support-leg posture correction. It does not establish general dynamic walking, arbitrary terrain handling, or recovery from all external pushes.

09Robotics & estimation2016

Can a robot infer contact forces without dedicated force sensors?

Separate body–leg coupling from environmental contact before mapping estimated torques to foot forces.

The problem

Joint measurements reflect both internal coupling and external contact. An observer that mixes these effects can misattribute the forces acting on a robot leg.

The main idea

Use two connected nonlinear disturbance observers. The first estimates coupling torques; the second uses that information to estimate external torques. A Jacobian maps the torque estimate to a force estimate using joint position and velocity measurements.

What the paper shows

The manuscript reports convergence properties for slowly varying torques, bounded-error behavior for faster changes, and simulation comparisons on the TMUBot model.

Scope & limitations

Force variation rate affects estimation accuracy. The reported validation is simulation-based; the result does not imply exact recovery of arbitrarily fast contact forces.
